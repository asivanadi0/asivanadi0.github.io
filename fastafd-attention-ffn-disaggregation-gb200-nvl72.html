When Does AFD Beat Wide EP on GB200 NVL72? Notes on FastAFD

6 November 2026 · Anpoo Sivanadi

Attention–FFN disaggregation (AFD) splits MoE decode so attention GPUs hold the KV cache and a separate FFN pool runs the experts. Wide expert parallelism (wide EP) keeps both on every GPU and shards the experts across the rack. A new paper, FastAFD: When Does Attention-FFN Disaggregation Pay on Rack-Scale NVLink?, measures both on one GB200 NVL72 rack against a carefully tuned vLLM wide-EP baseline. The code is at hao-ai-lab/FastAFD.

The useful claim is not "AFD is always faster". It is that, once you tune wide EP per workload, AFD's all-GPU win shrinks to about 1.00–1.17×, and you can predict which side of that range you are on from colocated profiles alone.

1. The rack and the models

One GB200 NVL72: 18 four-GPU nodes, 72 GPUs in a single NVLink domain. Decode only (prefill is assumed to come from a separate P/D pool). Three production FP8 MoE models:

Model Attention Total / active Layers Experts Top-K
MiniMax-M2.5 Full (GQA) 229B / 10B 62 256 8
Qwen3-235B-A22B Full (GQA) 235B / 22B 94 128 8
Qwen3.5-397B-A17B 15 full + 45 linear 397B / 17B 60 512 10

Contexts are 4K, 8K, and 16K. Prefill once, then measure steady-state decode. Experts stay in DeepSeek-style block FP8. FastAFD attention uses TensorRT-LLM FMHA; the FFN pool runs a MegaMoE path over NVLink. Each attention GPU's batch is split into two ping-pong microbatches so layer ℓ's remote experts for one microbatch run behind the other's attention.

2. The comparison that actually matters

Against single-node EP4, FastAFD looks dramatic: 1.30–2.36× all-GPU throughput. That is the wrong baseline for NVL72. Scaling colocated vLLM across the rack lifts the baseline by 22–114%, and the peak sits at interior widths EP16–EP32, not EP64. Once each engine runs at its measured best configuration, FastAFD's all-GPU speedup Sall is:

Model Ctx Best wide EP Best A:F SEP4 Sall Satt
MiniMax-M2.5 4K EP32 9:1 1.40 1.00 1.12
MiniMax-M2.5 8K EP32 15:1 1.52 1.02 1.09
MiniMax-M2.5 16K EP32 17:1† 1.56 1.02 1.08
Qwen3 4K EP16 5:1 1.30 1.06 1.28
Qwen3 8K EP16 7:1 1.41 1.10 1.26
Qwen3 16K EP32 11:1 1.42 1.17 1.28
Qwen3.5 4K EP32 5:1 2.25 1.16 1.39
Qwen3.5 8K EP32 5:1 2.36 1.10 1.33
Qwen3.5 16K EP32 5:1 2.11 0.99 1.19

† still rising at the 18-node rack limit. Sall counts every allocated GPU, including FFN nodes that serve no requests. Satt counts attention GPUs only.

Absolute numbers from Appendix Table A2 (tokens/s/GPU, all GPUs counted) make the same point concrete. At 16K, Qwen3 FastAFD at 11:1 hits 1452 tok/s/GPU against vLLM EP32 at 1240 (1.17×). MiniMax at 17:1 is 1234 against EP32 at 1208 (1.02×). Qwen3.5 at 5:1 is 3955 against EP32 at 3982 (0.99×).

3. Two gains and one cost

The paper factors the all-GPU result as:

Sall=bAbV⏟batch gain×TVTA⏟overlap gain×rr+1⏟disaggregation cost

where r=NA/NF is the attention:FFN node ratio, bA and bV are per-GPU decode batches, and TA, TV are decode step times.

  • Batch gain. Attention GPUs hold no expert weights, so more HBM goes to KV. Against tuned wide EP the leftover batch advantage is only about 1.06–1.16× on most workloads; wide EP has already reclaimed most of the memory gap by shrinking each GPU's expert shard. Qwen3.5 is the exception at 1.4–1.6×.
  • Overlap gain. Two-microbatch ping-pong hides remote MoE behind the other microbatch's attention. Heavy-expert Qwen3 (experts in all 94 layers) gains the most. Light-expert MiniMax barely moves the step. Qwen3.5 can even run a slower step than wide EP, because attention-side projections run twice per step and its short linear-attention layers leave less cover for remote FFN.
  • Disaggregation cost. Exactly r/(r+1): FFN nodes hold no KV and emit no tokens. At 5:1 that is 5/6 ≈ 0.83; at 11:1 it is 11/12 ≈ 0.92. Attention-GPU speedup Satt reaches 1.08–1.39×; the rack-level number is that figure times the cost.

AFD wins on all-GPU throughput only when the two gains outweigh the cost. That is why MiniMax sits at parity, Qwen3 keeps 1.06–1.17×, and Qwen3.5 falls from 1.16× at 4K to parity at 16K.

4. Speculative decoding changes the provisioning, not just the speedup

With draft depth d=3 (four verified tokens per step) and acceptance held near a¯≈1.8 for both engines, verifying multiplies MoE work. Under AFD that work lands on the FFN pool; under wide EP it lands on every GPU's critical path. Optimal A:F ratios contract by more than half (MiniMax from 9:1–17:1 down to 4:1–8:1; Qwen3 from 5:1–11:1 down to 2:1–5:1). Despite giving FFN a larger share, FastAFD lifts MiniMax Sall from parity up to 1.08× and holds Qwen3 at 1.10–1.13×, while Satt rises to 1.22–1.70× across workloads. The abstract's "further 8%" is this lift of MiniMax's all-GPU advantage over wide EP.

5. Decide before you deploy

The operational piece I care about most: a per-layer analytical model, calibrated only from colocated vLLM profiles (no AFD runs), predicts FastAFD throughput with 2.9% mean relative error across 65 deployments up to each workload's optimal ratio (Figure 6). Median error in standard decoding at or below the peak is 2.69%. Past the peak the model is less trustworthy (mean error about 10%), which is fine if you use it to pick a ratio rather than to extrapolate beyond it.

That turns AFD from a rewrite into a go/no-go check. Profile wide EP on the target rack, plug the numbers in, and see whether the predicted Sall clears the bar before you carve attention and FFN pools.

6. What still bounds homogeneous AFD

Two microbatches are enough for NVL72's NVLink round trip on these models, but they also force attention-side projections twice per step. Homogeneous GPU AFD cannot erase the disaggregation cost. The authors' ceiling with near-zero remote FFN latency (for example experts on SRAM-backed accelerators next to a Vera Rubin NVL72) raises the ideal speculative speedup as high as 2.48×. That is a different rack design, not a knob on today's homogeneous deployment.

Closing

My own opinion is that for MoE decode on a homogeneous GB200 NVL72, wide EP with a tuned EP width is the default, and AFD is a conditional upgrade for heavy-expert full-attention models at long context, especially once speculative decoding is on. The paper's value is the measurement against that default, plus a model that lets you skip the rewrite when the answer is no.
