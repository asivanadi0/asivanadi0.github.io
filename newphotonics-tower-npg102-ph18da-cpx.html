NewPhotonics NPG102 + Tower PH18DA: Laser-Integrated PICs Shipping for 800G–1.6T — and the CPX 6.4T On-Ramp

20 September 2026 · Anpoo Sivanadi · based on Tower Semiconductor / NewPhotonics PH18DA high-volume shipment announcement and Sep 17–18 Converge Digest / Semiconductor Today reads

NewPhotonics NPG102 + Tower PH18DA — laser-integrated PICs shipping for 800G–1.6T, plus the CPX 6.4T on-ramp.

My Lightmatter Passage L20 CPX note was about what BiDi near-package optics do to a 512-GPU fiber plant once Open CPX is on the table. This morning is the other half of that cabling story: who is actually shipping the laser-integrated photonic engines those modules need — and what rate, form factor, and MSA path they claim when the foundry says “high volume.”

On September 17, 2026, Tower Semiconductor and NewPhotonics announced high-volume shipment of laser-integrated, serviceable optical-engine PICs built on Tower’s PH18DA silicon photonics platform. Primary sources: the Tower press release, the GlobeNewswire mirror, Converge Digest’s Sep 17 read, and Semiconductor Today’s Sep 18 write-up. Specs below are vendor claims; treat “first on PH18DA” and roadmap dates as supplier language until OEMs publish BOM and bring-up data.

CPX here is the same Open CPX MSA socket language as Passage L20: optics next to the host ASIC / switch / accelerator package, not a faceplate transceiver fighting the same real estate as power and cooling. The difference in this note is supply — a named PIC SKU and foundry platform crossing from production orders into volume delivery.

What “laser-integrated” changes on the BOM

A conventional optical engine often treats the laser as a separate assembly problem: attach, align, qualify, and inventory an external source (or an ELSFP tray) next to a silicon photonics die that only does the passive and electro-optic half of the job. Heterogeneous InP-on-SiPho puts lasers and SOAs on the same PIC as modulators and detectors. Tower and NewPhotonics argue that cuts discrete optical interfaces, manufacturing variability, and time-to-yield — the boring reasons a capacity planner cares about a foundry PR.

Three operational consequences matter more than the “first on PH18DA” label:

  1. Serviceable NPO. The release repeatedly pairs “near-packaged” with socket pluggable and serviceable. That is the architectural compromise between permanent CPO attach (shortest electrical path, worst field-replace story) and faceplate pluggables (easy swap, longer SERDES). You want the OE next to the package for 200G-class lanes, and you still want to pull a failed engine without scrapping the switch or GPU tray.
  2. One PIC across FRO/LRO/LPO and NPO. Shipping the same laser-integrated engine family into front-panel reduced-optics modules and socketed NPO is how a supplier amortizes PH18DA yield across two deployment modes. For a neocloud BOM, that is a second-source and spare-parts argument, not a marketing taxonomy.
  3. 200G/lane today, 6.4T CPX tomorrow. NPG102 at 200G per lane for 800G–1.6T engines is the volume product. NPC505 at 6.4T under CPX-MSA in 1H27 is the scale-up socket story that sits on the same Open CPX whiteboard as Passage L20’s 6.4 Tbps directional claim. Do not collapse them: one is shipping; one is a dated volume plan.

Converge Digest’s useful context: in March they reported NewPhotonics had placed the first volume-production orders for 800G/1.6T PICs on PH18DA. Sep 17 is the step from order to shipment. That is a commercialization milestone, not a new architecture paper.

How this sits next to Lightmatter, Helios, and Cornelis

Lightmatter Passage L20 CPX is a module + BiDi fiber-plant story: 6.4 Tbps/direction, 32 lanes @ 212.5 Gbps PAM4, Open CPX Type 2, modeled 131k → 65k fibers on a 512-GPU pod. This NewPhotonics note does not reopen that BiDi arithmetic. It asks whether the PIC supply behind CPX-class NPO is real enough to put on a 2026–2027 interconnect BOM — NPG102 shipping, NPC505 CPX-MSA volume in 1H27.

Helios UALoE and the open scale-up networking lane (UALink / ESUN / Ethernet bridges) are about what protocol sits above the optics. Qualcomm × Amazon 1.6T optical was an earlier optical density foil on the inference side. Put NewPhotonics next to those as engine silicon and foundry, not as a substitute for UALink switch silicon or Lightmatter’s BiDi module math.

I would not read “energy-efficient” and “manufacturing consistency” in the PR as a substitute for pJ/bit or FIT rates. Those belong in OEM datasheets and bring-up reports after ECOC demos land.

Caveats I would write into any NPO supply ticket

  1. Vendor “first” and “high volume.” Tower’s claim that NewPhotonics is first on PH18DA into high-volume production is a foundry partnership statement. Ask for monthly ship rates and customer OEMs before treating it as industry share.
  2. 800G–1.6T ≠ 6.4T. NPG102 shipping rates are the lower-density engines. NPC505 6.4T is 1H27 planned volume — after Passage L20’s anticipated Q1 2027 EVK window on the Lightmatter side. Align calendars; do not assume concurrent availability.
  3. Lane math differs. Lightmatter’s L20 foil uses 212.5 Gbps PAM4 on 32 lanes for 6.4 Tbps/dir. NewPhotonics’ shipping NPG102 is 200G/lane for 800G–1.6T; the NPC505 color Converge Digest cites is 224 Gbps PAM4 on a DR32 engine. Different products, different lane tables — do not mix them in one CapEx model.
  4. MSA compliance is a socket claim. “CPX-MSA compliant” on NPC505 means the chipset targets the Open CPX mechanical/electrical envelope. It is not automatic interoperability with every Guide DR laser or every BiDi module on the MSA roster.
  5. No published pJ/bit or reach table in the HVM PR. Passage L20’s product page claims ~5 pJ/bit and >500 m SMF; this Tower/NewPhotonics release does not put matching efficiency or reach numbers on NPG102. Hold those cells empty until OEM modules publish them.
  6. ECOC is a demo, not a rack qualification. Sep 21–23 Malaga meetings are useful for samples and roadmap slides. They are not a substitute for hyperscaler optical bring-up on your switch ASIC.

How I would use this tomorrow morning

Put an NPG102 (shipping) / NPC505 6.4T CPX (1H27) row next to your Passage L20 CPX fiber model so engine supply and MSA socket timing sit beside BiDi fiber counts — not underneath them as a “optics will exist” assumption. If you are sizing FRO/LRO/LPO faceplate ports for scale-out while parking NPO for scale-up, ask whether one PH18DA-based PIC family can cover both spare pools. If you are already on NVLink Fusion or UALoE for the protocol layer, read this as optical-engine second-source intelligence for the CPX socket, not a reason to change the fabric.
