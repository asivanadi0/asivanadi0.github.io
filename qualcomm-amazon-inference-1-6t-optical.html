Qualcomm × Amazon: Custom Inference Silicon, 1.6T Optics, and a Second ASIC Line Next to Trainium

8 September 2026 · Anpoo Sivanadi · based on Qualcomm’s 8 Sep 2026 Amazon collaboration materials

Qualcomm × Amazon custom AI inference silicon and 1.6T optics — a second ASIC line next to Trainium, with the HBC and datasheet caveats.

Qualcomm and Amazon announced a multi-generation collaboration on custom AI inference silicon for large-scale AWS data centers, plus high-performance optical connectivity extending to 1.6 terabits per second. The SEC 8-K underneath the PR is the systems signal: Amazon can unlock equity only by actually buying chips and interconnect. That is not an earnings recap. It is a rack question — when does AWS want a second inference ASIC vendor next to Annapurna’s Trainium line, and what changes when the same partner co-designs the optical spine?

Spec sheet (what is named today)

Announcement Sep 8, 2026 — Qualcomm Technologies × Amazon / AWS
Compute scope Multi-generation customized silicon for AI inference (SKU FLOPS / HBM not disclosed)
Optical scope High-performance optical connectivity; solutions extending to 1.6 Tb/s; Qualcomm SerDes + optical DSP (Alphawave line)
Purchase envelope Up to about $60B of Qualcomm AI data-center chips / related products (Reuters / 8-K coverage)
Warrant Amazon right to buy up to 25M Qualcomm shares @ $161.26; vesting tied to purchases through ~2036; initial ~3.75M shares unlocked on starting commitments (HotHardware on the 8-K)
AWS context Amazon custom-chip business cited at >$25B annualized run-rate end of June quarter (Reuters)
Merchant roadmap (context, not the AWS SKU) Dragonfly AI250 HBC Gen 1: up to 133 TB/s “effective” BW/card (18× vs AI200 LPDDR); AI300 HBC Gen 2: 54× vs AI200; scale-up via UALink + ESUN; connectivity portfolio 800G / 1.6T
Prior interconnect buy Qualcomm’s ~$2.4B Alphawave Semi acquisition (Tony Pialis now data-center chip chief)
Parallel deal shape Marvell × Google custom AI + stake rights (weeks earlier, Reuters)

Primary sources: Qualcomm PR, Reuters, HotHardware / 8-K read, Dragonfly Investor Day, The Register on HBC.

What actually shipped as news

Two workstreams, one contract.

First: custom inference XPUs for AWS. Qualcomm’s public Dragonfly cards (AI200 / AI250 / AI300) are the architectural color, not a promise that Amazon is buying the merchant SKU unchanged. The PR is careful — “customized silicon at scale,” “working together on AI inference.” Until AWS publishes a Trainium-style NeuronCore table for the Qualcomm part, treat every FLOP claim as merchant roadmap, not BOM.

Second: optical connectivity to 1.6T. That is the named bandwidth you can put next to an UltraServer drawing. Qualcomm says the collaboration uses its SerDes and optical DSP — the Alphawave stack it bought to stop showing up to hyperscalers with only a mobile NPU story. Campus-reach and AOC/AEC product language on the June Dragonfly deck already spanned 800G and 1.6T; today’s deal is Amazon writing that interconnect into a multi-year AWS build.

The financing shape matters for operators even if you never buy a Qualcomm share. Vesting against product purchases means Amazon’s incentive is to consume silicon and optics, not to sit on a paper option. The $60B ceiling is an upper bound through 2036, not a 2026 PO. Still: a second inference supplier with a purchase-tied warrant is a different signal than a slide about “exploring custom.”

Why a second inference line next to Trainium

AWS already has Annapurna. Trainium3 UltraServers are in market; Trainium4 is the part that picks up NVLink Fusion so Amazon XPUs can sit on the same scale-up fabric as NVIDIA GPUs — the story I wrote when NVHBM landed. That is the “heterogeneous rack with NVIDIA’s switch” path.

Qualcomm is the other bet: power-efficient decode and a connectivity vendor that can also ship the SerDes/DSP for the optical plant. AWS’s published custom-chip run-rate (>$25B annualized) already proves captive silicon is a business, not a lab. Adding Qualcomm does not retire Trainium. It segments the floor the way every hyperscaler is segmenting:

  • Training / general GPU — still mostly NVIDIA (and some AMD).
  • First-party training+inference XPU — Trainium / Inferentia family.
  • Additional inference-optimized custom — today’s Qualcomm lane, SKU TBD.
  • Scale-up / scale-out — NeuronLink inside the UltraServer; Ethernet / optics between; and now a co-designed 1.6T optical path with the same partner building the accelerator.

If you run multi-cloud capacity planning, the useful question is not “will Qualcomm unseat CUDA.” It is which ISL/OSL shapes AWS will pin to the Qualcomm part once a SKU sheet exists — likely the memory-bandwidth-bound decode jobs where HBM TCO hurts and a non-HBM or HBC-style package looks attractive on tokens per watt.

The HBC asterisk (merchant roadmap, not AWS silicon)

I am not going to pretend Qualcomm published peak PFLOPS for AI250/AI300. They did not. The Register called that out in June, and the “effective” bandwidth language is load-bearing marketing.

What is named on the merchant deck:

  • AI250 / HBC Gen 1 — up to 133 TB/s effective memory bandwidth per card; Qualcomm frames that as 18× versus AI200’s LPDDR5X baseline.
  • AI300 / HBC Gen 2 — another step to 54× versus AI200 on the same “effective” axis; sampling language points at 2028 for that generation.
  • Architecture bet — 3D-stack compute under or beside DRAM so low-arithmetic-intensity ops never pay a full SoC hop; Qualcomm claims roughly 6× bandwidth-per-watt versus HBM at card level (vendor estimate).
  • Scale-up — UALink and ESUN (Ethernet for Scale-Up Networking) on the Dragonfly racks — the same open-scale-up family I wrote about for AMD Helios UALoE, where native UALink switch silicon is still catching up and Ethernet bridges fill 2026.

Use those numbers as architecture color for why Amazon might want Qualcomm in the tent. Do not paste 133 TB/s into an AWS capacity spreadsheet and call it the new Inferentia. The Sep 8 deal is custom. Custom means the BOM can diverge.

Against Groq 3 LPX the contrast is clean: LPX sells deterministic SRAM decode with a third-party 3,431 tok/s at 100K and a batch-1 asterisk. Qualcomm sells (on the merchant side) LPDDR/HBC capacity and “effective” bandwidth multipliers with no public tok/s suite. Different proof standards. Different buyer.

Optics are half the product

People will screenshot the warrant and miss the interconnect.

AI racks are increasingly limited by how you move activations and KV between prefill and decode islands, not by whether the accelerator brochure has one more PFLOP. Co-designing 1.6T optical with the same vendor building the inference XPU is AWS saying the network SKU is in the same multi-year conversation as the compute SKU. That matches how NVIDIA ships NVLink Switch + GPU as one platform, and how Helios talks UALink even while shipping UALoE.

Qualcomm’s June connectivity portfolio already spanned die-to-die, copper, optical, and campus-reach up to ~20 km, with PAM4 and coherent-lite DSP language. Alphawave is why that paragraph is credible. The Amazon deal is why it is no longer only a slide for ByteDance and Middle East sovereign builds.

Caveats I would write into any AWS BOM note

  1. No public AWS Qualcomm XPU datasheet yet. No FLOPS, no HBM/LPDDR capacity, no TDP, no Neuron SDK support matrix. Plan with a placeholder SKU, not a fake table.
  2. “Effective” bandwidth ≠ pin bandwidth. Register’s skepticism on the AI200/AI250 multipliers still applies until independent card measurements land.
  3. $60B is a ceiling through 2036, not a 2026 ship plan. Vesting math is interesting; delivery schedules are what fill a floor.
  4. Trainium4 + NVLink Fusion remains the heterogeneous-GPU path. Qualcomm is additive inference/optical, not a replacement announcement for Annapurna.
  5. Marvell × Google shows the deal template is spreading. Custom silicon + equity rights is becoming how hyperscalers underwrite second-source ASIC vendors — watch the SKU sheet, not the warrant headline.

Bottom line

On September 8, 2026, Qualcomm and Amazon put a named 1.6T optical collaboration and a purchase-tied warrant next to a multi-generation custom AI inference silicon program for AWS. The architectural bet you can defend today sits on Qualcomm’s merchant Dragonfly/HBC roadmap and Alphawave SerDes/DSP — LPDDR/HBC-style memory bandwidth per watt, UALink/ESUN scale-up, and optics in the same contract as compute.

Next to the live posts on NVHBM / Trainium4 Fusion, Helios UALoE, and Groq 3 LPX, the early-September systems picture is consistent: hyperscalers are buying option sets — open scale-up, proprietary decode accelerators, captive ASICs, and now a second inference vendor with the optical plant in the same PO language.

When AWS publishes the Qualcomm XPU table, rewrite the FLOPS row. Until then, the operator takeaway is the dual scope: inference silicon + 1.6T optics, purchase-tied, multi-generation — not a smartphone diversification press release.
